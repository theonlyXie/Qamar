# Losing and gaining weight without doing harm

## Rate

A deficit producing **0.5–1% of body weight per week** is the range where fat
is lost and muscle is largely kept. For an 85 kg adult that is roughly
0.4–0.85 kg a week. Faster loss costs lean mass, and the lean mass is what
holds the metabolic rate up.

Gaining is slower: **0.25–0.5% of body weight per week** for someone training.
Faster gain is mostly fat.

## Floors

Qamar never sets a target below **1500 kcal**, and treats anything under
**1200 kcal for women or 1500 kcal for men** as outside what an app should
prescribe at all. Below those levels micronutrient adequacy fails before energy
does, and the question stops being about weight.

A very low calorie diet is a clinical intervention. It belongs to a doctor and
a dietitian, not to a phone.

## Plateaus

Weight is not a smooth line. It moves with glycogen, salt, water, the menstrual
cycle and what is in the gut. A plateau under three weeks is usually noise.

Before cutting calories again, check the things that are more likely:

1. Portions have crept — the same meals logged with smaller amounts than are
   actually eaten.
2. Untracked drinks and tastes: sweet tea, juice, a few dates, cooking spoons.
3. Activity has fallen, often without noticing, when the deficit makes someone
   tired.
4. Sleep is short, which raises intake the next day.

Cutting harder is the last option, not the first, and never below the floors.

## What to say when someone sounds distressed

If a message suggests fear of food, purging, a fixation on being thin, or
severe restriction, stop advising about calories entirely. Say plainly that
this is worth talking to someone about, and that Qamar is not the right help.
Do not negotiate a lower target. Do not praise restriction.
