# Egyptian dishes broken into ingredients

No food database contains "koshari". USDA has ingredients, Open Food Facts has
packaged products; a cooked national dish is neither. So this document gives
each dish as the ingredients it is built from and the amount of each in a
normal serving, and the energy is computed from the database figures for those
ingredients.

The amounts below are typical Cairo home and street portions. They are
starting points to be confirmed with the person, not measurements of their
particular plate. When someone says "a medium koshary", this is what that
usually means; when they say "large", assume roughly 1.5×.

---

## Foul medames (فول مدمس)

The default Egyptian breakfast. Slow-cooked dried fava beans, served with the
cooking liquid, dressed at the table.

**Typical plate (one person):**
- Cooked fava beans, 180 g
- Vegetable or olive oil, 1 tbsp (15 ml) — often more from a street cart
- Lemon juice, cumin, salt — negligible energy
- Sometimes: chopped tomato 40 g, onion 20 g, a boiled egg

Eaten with 1–2 baladi loaves. The oil is the variable that matters: a
tablespoon is 120 kcal and a generous street ladle can be double.

USDA lookup: "Broadbeans (fava beans), mature seeds, cooked".

## Taameya (طعمية)

Egyptian falafel, made from **dried split fava beans**, not chickpeas — this
matters for both composition and for anyone comparing against Levantine
falafel figures.

**Typical serving:** 4 pieces, about 25 g each raw-formed, deep-fried.

Deep-frying is the dominant term. Fried food absorbs roughly 8–12% of its
weight in oil, so 100 g of taameya carries something like 10 g of absorbed oil
— about 90 kcal on top of the beans. Treat any taameya estimate as low
confidence unless the person knows how it was fried.

## Koshary (كشري)

Rice, lentils and pasta together, topped with chickpeas, fried onion and a
tomato sauce, with cumin-vinegar (daqqa) and chilli (shatta) added at the
table.

**Medium bowl (طبق وسط), roughly 400–450 g total:**
- Cooked white rice, 150 g
- Cooked brown lentils, 90 g
- Cooked small macaroni, 90 g
- Cooked chickpeas, 40 g
- Tomato sauce, 60 g
- Fried onions, 15 g
- Oil carried in the sauce and onions, 1–2 tbsp

A small bowl is roughly two thirds of this; a large is roughly 1.5×; the
takeaway bucket sold as "family" is 3–4 servings and is very often eaten by
one or two people.

The fried onion and the oil are where the energy hides — the base is otherwise
a reasonable high-carbohydrate, moderate-protein meal, and the lentils and
chickpeas make its protein better than pasta alone.

## Molokhia (ملوخية)

Finely chopped jute mallow leaves cooked in broth, finished with garlic fried
in ghee (taqleya). Served over rice, with chicken, rabbit or beef alongside.

**Typical serving:** 200 g cooked molokhia + 150 g cooked rice + the protein.

The leaves themselves are low in energy and high in micronutrients. The ghee in
the taqleya and the fat on the meat are the terms that count. Note it as a
vegetable-forward meal whose energy is decided by what is served with it.

## Mahshi (محشي)

Vegetables — courgette (kousa), aubergine, cabbage leaves, vine leaves, peppers
— stuffed with seasoned rice and herbs, cooked in tomato broth.

**Typical serving:** 5–6 pieces, roughly 60 g each.

Effectively a rice dish in a vegetable wrapper. The rice is usually mixed with
oil before stuffing, so a serving carries 1–2 tbsp of oil that is not visible
on the plate.

## Bamia (بامية)

Okra stewed in tomato sauce, usually with lamb or beef.

**Typical serving:** 250 g stew, meat included, with rice.

## Fattah (فتة)

Layered: toasted or fried baladi bread, rice, meat, and a garlic-vinegar tomato
sauce, often with ghee poured over. Eid and celebration food.

**Typical serving:** 300–400 g, and energy-dense at every layer — fried bread,
buttered rice, fatty meat, ghee. This is a dish to plan a day around rather
than to fit into an ordinary one.

## Hawawshi (حواوشي)

Spiced minced meat baked inside baladi bread.

**Typical serving:** one loaf, containing 100–150 g minced meat.

Ask about the meat's fat content — Egyptian mince is commonly fatty, and the
difference between lean and standard mince is roughly 100 kcal per 100 g.

## Shawarma and grilled meats

Kofta, shish tawook, kebab. **Typical serving:** 150–200 g cooked meat, with
bread, salad, tahina.

Grilled protein is where a target is easiest to hit. The tahina, the bread and
the chips beside it are where it is missed — tahina is roughly 90 kcal per
tablespoon.

## Feteer meshaltet (فطير مشلتت)

Laminated pastry made with large amounts of ghee, sweet or savoury.

**Typical serving:** a quarter of a standard feteer.

Very energy-dense: the layers are built with ghee brushed between them. Treat
as an occasion food and say so plainly rather than trying to fit it in.

## Roz bel laban and om ali

Milk-and-rice pudding; and bread-and-milk pastry pudding with nuts and raisins.

**Typical serving:** 150–200 g. Both carry sugar plus full-fat milk or cream,
and om ali adds pastry and nuts.

# Everyday components

## Baladi bread (عيش بلدي)

The staple, wholemeal, government-subsidised. **One loaf ≈ 90 g, about 250
kcal**, with useful fibre. Two loaves with breakfast is normal and is not a
problem in itself — it is 500 kcal that needs to be in the plan rather than
outside it.

Shami (white) bread is lighter in fibre; a loaf is smaller, around 45–60 g.

## Gebna qareesh (جبنة قريش)

Fresh skimmed cheese, high protein, low fat. The single most useful food for an
Egyptian trying to raise protein without raising cost — cheaper than meat and
available everywhere.

**Typical serving:** 60–100 g.

Gebna roumi and processed triangles are the opposite: much higher fat and salt.

## Tea with sugar (شاي)

Standard glass, 2–3 teaspoons of sugar, 5–8 times a day is common. At 16 kcal
per teaspoon, **six glasses with three sugars each is roughly 290 kcal a day**
and comfortably breaches WHO's free-sugar limit before any food is counted.

This is the highest-yield single change available to most Egyptian users, and
it costs them nothing. Reducing by one spoon per glass is achievable; asking
for unsweetened tea usually is not.

## Dates (بلح, تمر)

Fresh or dried. **A dried date ≈ 20 kcal**, mostly sugar with useful fibre and
potassium. Central to Ramadan and to hospitality.

## Sugarcane juice, karkade, sobia, tamr hindi

Street drinks. Sugarcane juice is essentially liquid sugar — a standard glass
carries 200 kcal or more. Karkade (hibiscus) is negligible unsweetened and
significant sweetened. Ask how it is sweetened before estimating anything.
