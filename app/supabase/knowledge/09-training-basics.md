# Training that survives contact with a real life

## Progressive overload

Muscle and strength come from asking for slightly more over time — more weight,
more repetitions, more sets, or better control of the same load. Doing the same
session at the same difficulty indefinitely maintains, it does not build.

Add small amounts. A 2.5 kg jump on a lift that is already hard is a large
jump; an extra repetition is often the better step.

## Frequency beats duration

Two or three sessions a week that actually happen beat five planned sessions
that do not. For a beginner, two full-body sessions a week produce most of the
available progress.

## Recovery is part of training

Adaptation happens between sessions, not during them. A muscle group trained
hard wants roughly 48 hours. Sleep is the largest single recovery variable and
the one most people ignore in favour of supplements that matter far less.

## Soreness is not the goal

Delayed onset muscle soreness tracks novelty, not effectiveness. A session that
leaves someone unable to walk is not a better session; it is one that cost them
the next two.

## Cardio and lifting together

They are not in conflict at the volumes anybody here is doing. Fat loss is
decided by the energy deficit; resistance training is what makes the weight
lost come mostly from fat rather than muscle. Someone in a deficit should be
lifting, not only walking.

## Training in a deficit

Expect performance to stall or dip. Keep the intensity, cut the volume if
needed, keep protein high (see the requirements document). This is a period to
hold ground, not to set records.

## What to tell a complete beginner

1. Walk daily, and build toward the WHO 150 minutes.
2. Two full-body resistance sessions a week: a push, a pull, a squat or hinge,
   and something for the core. Bodyweight is a legitimate starting point.
3. Add one repetition or a small amount of weight when a set feels controlled.
4. Sleep, and eat the protein.

Anything more complicated than this is a distraction until those four are
habits.

## Where the app stops

Programming around an injury, rehabilitation, and any pain that is sharp,
one-sided or persistent are not nutrition or training questions. Say so and
suggest a physiotherapist or a doctor.
