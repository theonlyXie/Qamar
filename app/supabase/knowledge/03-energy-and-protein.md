# How a daily target is arrived at

## Resting energy: Mifflin-St Jeor

Qamar estimates resting metabolic rate with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which
is the one that performs best in validation studies against indirect
calorimetry in non-obese and obese adults:

```
RMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age(years) + s
s = +5 for men, −161 for women
```

The sex constant is not cosmetic. Applying the male constant to a woman
overstates her requirement by 166 kcal every single day — enough to explain
away a real plateau as a failure of effort.

An equation is an estimate. Two people of the same height, weight, age and sex
can differ by 10% or more in reality. The target is a starting point to be
adjusted against what the scale and the logbook actually do over weeks.

## Activity factor

Total daily energy is RMR multiplied by an activity factor:

- **1.2** — sedentary: desk work, little walking
- **1.375** — light: some walking, light exercise 1–3 days a week
- **1.55** — moderate: exercise 3–5 days a week, or a job on your feet
- **1.725** — high: hard exercise 6–7 days a week
- **1.9** — very high: physical labour plus training

Most people overestimate their own category. When someone is between two,
assume the lower and let the data correct it.

## Protein

The population reference intake for a healthy adult is **0.83 g per kg of body
weight per day** (FAO/WHO/UNU). That is a floor for avoiding deficiency, not an
optimum for someone training or dieting.

Higher intakes are supported where the goal is body composition:

- **1.2–1.6 g/kg/day** for a general active adult, or anyone in an energy
  deficit, where the aim is to keep muscle while losing fat.
- **1.6–2.2 g/kg/day** for someone training for strength or size. There is
  little evidence of further benefit above roughly 2.2 g/kg.

Qamar targets 1.8 g/kg by default, which sits inside the deficit-protective
range and is achievable on Egyptian food — foul, eggs, cottage-style gebna
qareesh, chicken, tuna, and lentils across the day.

For a healthy adult with healthy kidneys, intakes in this range are not
harmful. **For anyone with kidney disease this is a medical question and the
app must not answer it.**

## Fat and carbohydrate

After protein is set, Qamar allocates roughly 28% of energy to fat and gives
the remainder to carbohydrate. Fat below about 20% of energy makes the diet
hard to eat and risks fat-soluble vitamin intake; carbohydrate carries the
training and the culture — bread, rice and pasta are what Egyptian meals are
built on, and a plan that fights that is a plan nobody follows.
