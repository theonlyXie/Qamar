# How much activity an adult needs

From the WHO 2020 guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour.

## Aerobic activity

Adults aged 18–64 should do **150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic
activity per week**, or **75–150 minutes of vigorous-intensity**, or an
equivalent combination.

Going beyond 300 minutes moderate (or 150 vigorous) brings additional health
benefit.

Moderate intensity means you can talk but not sing — brisk walking, cycling on
the flat, most housework done at pace. Vigorous means you cannot say more than
a few words without a breath — running, football, stairs, carrying loads.

## Muscle strengthening

**On 2 or more days a week**, at moderate or greater intensity, working all
major muscle groups. This is a separate requirement, not a substitute for the
aerobic minutes, and it is the part most people skip.

## Sedentary behaviour

Limit time spent sitting. Replacing sedentary time with activity of any
intensity, including light, brings benefit.

## The messages that matter more than the numbers

- **Some activity is better than none.** Someone doing nothing gets more
  benefit from their first 30 minutes a week than an active person gets from
  their last hour.
- **Start small and build up.** Duration and frequency first, intensity later.
- **All activity counts.** Walking to the market, taking stairs, carrying
  shopping. It does not have to happen in a gym, and in Egypt for most people
  it will not.
