# What a healthy diet contains, in numbers

These are the World Health Organization's population-level targets for adults.
They are the baseline any specific advice has to be consistent with.

## Energy balance

Energy intake should balance energy expenditure. Unhealthy weight gain is the
consequence of a sustained excess, not of any single food.

## Fat

Total fat should be **30% or less of total daily energy intake**. Within that:

- **Saturated fat: no more than 10% of total energy.**
- **Trans fat: no more than 1% of total energy, of any type.** Industrially
  produced trans fat is not part of a healthy diet at any level and WHO calls
  for its elimination from the food supply.

Shifting fat intake away from saturated and trans fats toward unsaturated fats
is the change that matters most. In an Egyptian kitchen the practical version
is: less ghee and less deep-frying, more olive oil and more sesame.

## Free sugars

Free sugars should be **less than 10% of total daily energy intake** — about
**50 g, or roughly 12 level teaspoons**, for someone eating 2000 kcal. A
further reduction to **below 5%** brings additional benefit.

"Free sugars" means sugar added to food or drink plus the sugar naturally
present in honey, syrups and fruit juice. It does not mean the sugar inside
whole fruit or in plain milk. Three glasses of sweet tea in a day can reach the
10% limit on their own.

## Salt

**Less than 5 g of salt per day**, which is **2 g of sodium**. Most of it
arrives through bread, cheese, processed meat and pickles rather than through
the salt shaker. Iodised salt should be used where salt is used at all.

## Potassium

**At least 90 mmol/day (3510 mg/day)** may be beneficial, largely through
fruit and vegetables.

## Fruit and vegetables

**At least 400 g per day**, not counting potatoes and other starchy roots.
That is roughly five portions.

## Fibre

**At least 25 g of naturally occurring dietary fibre per day.**

## Protein

**10–15% of total daily energy is generally sufficient for adults** — around
**50–75 g** for someone of healthy body weight eating about 2000 kcal. Higher
intakes are relevant for people training hard or losing weight; see the
requirements document.

## Carbohydrate

Roughly **45–75% of total daily energy**, preferring whole grains, pulses,
vegetables and whole fruit over refined starch and sugar.
